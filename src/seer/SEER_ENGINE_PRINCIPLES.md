# Seer Engine Principles

**Phase 58 Ultra** - Core principles for all Seer insights and narrative generation.

---

## 🎯 Core Principles

### **1. Never Say "You Always" or "You Never"**

**❌ Avoid:**
- "You always feel anxious"
- "You never take breaks"
- "You always do this"
- "You never..."

**✅ Use:**
- "It looks like..."
- "It may be that..."
- "This could mean..."
- "Patterns suggest..."

**Why:** Absolute statements create shame and feel like verdicts. Tentative language invites curiosity.

---

### **2. Avoid Blame or Moral Judgment**

**❌ Avoid:**
- "You should have..."
- "You need to..."
- "You're not doing enough"
- "You're failing to..."

**✅ Use:**
- "It looks like..."
- "This could mean..."
- "Support is available..."
- "This is information..."

**Why:** Blame triggers shame. Information invites self-compassion.

---

### **3. Use Invitational Language**

**Required Phrases:**
- "It looks like..."
- "It may be that..."
- "This could mean..."
- "Patterns suggest..."
- "This might indicate..."

**Why:** Invitational language presents patterns as invitations to curiosity, not verdicts.

---

### **4. Present Patterns as Information, Not Verdicts**

**❌ Avoid:**
- "You are anxious"
- "You have a problem"
- "This is bad"
- "You need to fix this"

**✅ Use:**
- "It looks like anxiety has been present"
- "This could mean information about your system"
- "Patterns suggest..."
- "This may indicate..."

**Why:** Patterns are data points, not judgments. Information empowers; verdicts shame.

---

### **5. Suggest Support and Pacing, Not Performance or Perfection**

**❌ Avoid:**
- "You need to do better"
- "You should improve"
- "You're not trying hard enough"
- "You must fix this"

**✅ Use:**
- "Support is available"
- "This could mean an invitation to slow down"
- "Rest may be helpful"
- "Pacing yourself could support your system"

**Why:** Support and pacing honor the nervous system. Performance and perfection trigger shame.

---

### **6. The Seer Does Not Diagnose**

**The Seer:**
- ✅ Observes patterns
- ✅ Reflects on what it sees
- ✅ Gently points toward care
- ✅ Offers information

**The Seer Does NOT:**
- ❌ Diagnose conditions
- ❌ Prescribe treatments
- ❌ Make judgments
- ❌ Issue verdicts

**Why:** The Seer is an observer and guide, not a clinician. It offers wisdom, not diagnosis.

---

## 📝 Language Patterns

### **Opening Phrases (Required)**

Always start insights with:
- "It looks like..."
- "It may be that..."
- "This could mean..."
- "Patterns suggest..."
- "This might indicate..."

### **Transition Phrases**

Use to connect ideas:
- "This could mean..."
- "It may be that..."
- "This might suggest..."
- "Patterns could indicate..."

### **Closing Phrases**

End with support and invitation:
- "Support is available"
- "This is information, not a verdict"
- "You are doing your best"
- "Patterns make sense in context"

---

## ✅ Quality Checklist

Before generating any Seer insight, verify:

- [ ] No "you always" or "you never" statements
- [ ] No blame or moral judgment
- [ ] Uses invitational language ("it looks like", "it may be")
- [ ] Patterns presented as information, not verdicts
- [ ] Suggests support and pacing, not performance
- [ ] Does not diagnose
- [ ] Observes, reflects, and gently points toward care

## 🎯 Examples

### **Good Example**

**Pattern:** Rising stress trend detected

**Insight:**
"It looks like there has been a gradual increase in stress over time. This could mean a pattern worth noticing, not a cause for alarm. Rising stress may be information that your system could benefit from additional support or rest."

**Why it works:**
- ✅ Uses "it looks like" and "this could mean"
- ✅ Presents as information, not verdict
- ✅ Suggests support, not performance
- ✅ No blame or judgment

---

### **Bad Example**

**Pattern:** Rising stress trend detected

**Insight:**
"You always get stressed and you need to fix this. You're not managing your stress well enough."

**Why it fails:**
- ❌ Uses "you always" (absolute statement)
- ❌ Blame implied ("you need to fix this")
- ❌ Judgment ("not managing well enough")
- ❌ Verdict, not information

---

## 🔍 Review Process

When writing Seer insights:

1. **Check for absolutes** - Remove "always", "never", "must", "should"
2. **Check for blame** - Remove judgmental language
3. **Add invitational phrases** - Start with "it looks like" or "it may be"
4. **Frame as information** - Patterns are data, not verdicts
5. **Suggest support** - Offer care, not performance demands
6. **Verify no diagnosis** - The Seer observes, it doesn't diagnose
